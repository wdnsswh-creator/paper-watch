Here, we combined field observations along a natural salinity gradient with spectroscopic and mineralogical analyses, mineral sorption–desorption experiments, and manipulative incubations to examine the stability of mineral-protected OC. Salt-marsh sediments contained 9.23% less OC than freshwater sediments, with a disproportionate difference in reactive iron-bound OC (FeR-OC). Across the 36 independent field plots, FeR-OC represented 9.80–22.95% of total OC, while the FW–SM FeR-OC difference was numerically equivalent to 64.28% of the corresponding bulk-OC difference. This disproportionate response was associated with a shift from reactive Fe(III)-bearing phases toward Fe(II)-rich products, as well as a weakening of mineral protection, supported by spectroscopic, mineralogical, and geochemical evidence.
