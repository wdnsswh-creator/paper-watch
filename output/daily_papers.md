- 摘要核心内容：The response of bacterioplankton structure to salinity level in coastal lakes (n = 9) along the southern Baltic Sea coastline was studied. In terms of mean salinity levels (0.2−5.2 PSU), the lakes represented freshwater, transitional, and brackish types. Results showed that salinity determines the spatial and seasonal distribution patterns of microorganisms in costal lakes. Increased salinity contributed to a significant decline in total bacterial numbers (TBN). The TBN was lowest in brackish lakes in autumn (4 × 106 cells/mL) and highest in freshwater lakes in summer (7.11 × 106 cells/mL). The groups of Proteobacteria are appropriate bioindicators in any classifications of coastal ecosystems, particularly at low-haline stress. Alpha- and Gamma- subclasses of Proteobacteria are identifiers for brackish habitats, while Betaproteobacteria, due to their intolerance to haline stress, prefer freshwater habitats. Counts of euryhaline Actinobacteria, the dominant group of bacterioplankton (31.8%), decreased significantly with increased salinity.
